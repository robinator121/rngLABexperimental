## Answer in brief

**H1-C is NOT SUPPORTED in any of the four fault families.** No cell is an H1 cell, and no cell shows undetected harm. The verdicts are interpretable: I-1 to I-4, V1 and V2 all pass, and the independent recount agrees on 319 of 319 comparisons.

| Family | Frozen verdict | Cell classes (6 cells each) |
|---|---|---|
| SHIFT | NOT SUPPORTED | 2 conventional first, 2 indeterminate, 2 both detect and harmed |
| LAG1 | NOT SUPPORTED | 1 conventional first, 4 indeterminate, 1 both detect and harmed |
| DUP | NOT SUPPORTED | 4 conventional first, 1 indeterminate, 1 both detect and harmed |
| GFSR | NOT SUPPORTED | 6 harmless and quiet |

**What happened, as a negative result of equal weight:**
- **In the three synthetic families, the conventional battery alarmed after onset in every run of all 18 cells.** That includes the 7 "conventional first" cells, where the application's wrong-rate stayed below the class bound of 0.2 (observed 0.027 to 0.113, against 0.056 for healthy runs).
- **The application arm never alarmed in a run the battery missed** (0 such runs in 18 cells). When both arms alarmed, the application arm was later in every cell: the median delay ranged from 512 to 5,376 sweeps.
- So the second condition of H1-C, a quiet battery, failed in every synthetic cell. No level of harm could have produced an H1 cell there.
- **The real generator family caused no detectable harm.** All six GFSR lag pairs, including R250 (250, 103), were harmless and quiet. Their wrong-rate was 0.027 to 0.066, and both arms alarmed at about the false-alarm level.

Of the three outcomes the protocol gives the same prominence as an H1 cell (§1), two occurred: **conventional first** (7 cells) and **no harm in range** (the GFSR family). **Undetected harm** did not occur.

## 1. Primary endpoint: cell classes

The full per-cell table is T2 in `results/report_tables.md`. Figure 1 (`figures/fig1_rates_by_severity.png`) shows the three rates against severity, with 95% intervals.

**The battery alarmed in every run of every synthetic cell.** In SHIFT, LAG1 and DUP, the C-alarm count was 256 of 256 in each of the 18 cells. Its Bonferroni interval was [0.973533, 1] in each case.

**Conventional first** (C-alarm lower bound > 0.5 and wrong-rate upper bound < 0.2):
- the cells are SHIFT-0 and -1, LAG1-0, and DUP-0 to -3;
- their wrong-rates ran from 0.027 to 0.113.

Here the battery alarmed in every run while the wrong-rate stayed below the class bound. This is not equivalence to healthy coverage: SHIFT-0, at 0.113, is about twice the healthy rate. Harm is assessed over the fixed post-onset window, not at the moment the battery alarms.

**Indeterminate:**
- **The cells:** SHIFT-2 and -3, LAG1-1 to -4, and DUP-4.
- **Why:** in all seven the battery alarmed in every run, but the wrong-rate was intermediate (0.137 to 0.594). It was too high for "conventional first" and too low for "both detect, harmed", which needs a Bonferroni lower bound above 0.5.
- **The closest case is LAG1-4.** Its wrong-rate was 0.59375, but the lower bound was 0.49583. Here "indeterminate" describes the level of harm, not detection.

**Both detect and harmed:** SHIFT-4 and -5, LAG1-5 and DUP-5, with wrong-rates from 0.609 to 1.

**GFSR, harmless and quiet:**
- **Harm:** the wrong-rates were 0.027 to 0.066, and the largest 95% upper bound was 0.104 (`report_numbers.json`). Healthy runs were wrong at a rate of 0.056.
- **Alarms:** C alarmed in 0.035 to 0.059 of runs and A in 0.027 to 0.0625, against the calibrated 0.05.
- **Broken but undetected:** each GFSR cell has 6 to 16 runs that were wrong while the battery stayed quiet (`pass_but_broken`). Such runs are expected without any fault: the runs are wrong at about the healthy rate, and the battery is quiet at its calibrated rate.

## 2. Why the battery wins: which test fired first

Secondary endpoint; descriptive. Counts are summed over each family's six cells (`report_numbers.json`).

| Family | First conventional test to fire after onset | SP 800-90B health tests: post-onset alarm runs per cell (of 256) |
|---|---|---|
| SHIFT | SP 800-22 tests only: serial m = 8 first statistic 825 runs, approximate entropy 698, longest run 10, serial second statistic 3 | 256 in every cell |
| LAG1 | lag-1 word correlation in all 1,536 runs | 22 to 31 |
| DUP | adjacent identical words in all 1,536 runs | 21 to 23 in DUP-0 to -4 (T5); 248 in DUP-5 |
| healthy | spread over the SP 800-22 statistics and the lag-1 correlation; adjacent identical words never fired first (T5) | 365 of 4,096 (0.089) |

- **LAG1 and DUP were caught by the two word-level tests aimed at exactly these faults.** The author chose this battery in the protocol (§10, choice 2) because the smoke showed that the three in-repository tests barely react to LAG1 or DUP. A battery without the targeted tests would have been a straw man. For LAG1 and DUP, therefore, the result is about a battery that contains a test aimed at the fault. It does not show that SP 800-22 alone would detect them first, and that was not measured.
- **For SHIFT, standard SP 800-22 tests detected the fault first** in every run.
- **The SP 800-90B health tests alone would have missed LAG1 and most of DUP.** For LAG1, and for DUP up to severity 0.19, they alarmed at about their healthy rate. Their healthy rate (0.089) was not calibrated to 0.05, so this column is descriptive only.

## 3. Latency

Figure 2 (`figures/fig2_latency.png`) and T4 show these latencies.

- **SHIFT, LAG1 and DUP:** among runs where both arms detected, the median delay of A behind C was positive in every cell. It ranged from 512 to 5,376 sweeps. In bytes the median delay ran from 106,692 (DUP) to 1,137,484 (SHIFT). No run in these 18 cells had A detecting alone.
- **The highest-severity cell of each family has its smallest median delay:** 768 sweeps for SHIFT-5 and LAG1-5, and 512 for DUP-5. The trend is not monotone in between: from SHIFT-0 to SHIFT-1, for example, the delay rises from 2,304 to 5,376 sweeps.
- **GFSR:** both arms detected in only 2 runs each of GFSR-0 and GFSR-2, and in no run of the other four cells. The negative medians in Figure 2 for those two cells rest on 2 runs each, consistent with false alarms; they say nothing about detection order.

## 6. Secondary findings (descriptive, no gates)

### 6.1 The application signal is graded; the battery signal is saturated

- **The battery was saturated.** Across the 18 synthetic cells, C alarmed in every run at every severity.
- **The application arm's alarm rate rose with severity, roughly with the wrong-rate** (Figure 1). For example, it went from 0.035 to 1 across SHIFT and from 0.156 to 1 across LAG1.
- **The two monitors taken separately (T5).** Each has its own calibrated threshold, so these counts do not say which monitor triggered a combined-arm alarm.
  - In LAG1, A2 (the uphill-proposal fraction) alarmed far more often than A1, which stayed near its healthy level.
  - In SHIFT and at high DUP severity, both alarmed often.
  - In DUP-3, A1 alarmed more often than A2.

This is an observation, not a tested claim. It suggests a monitor that tracks harm may carry information about remaining lifetime that a saturated alarm cannot (Section 9).

### 6.2 The shape monitor and the CUSUM

Both are pre-registered secondaries, each with its own threshold on the same healthy runs (T5). Healthy runs alarmed 182 (shape) and 183 (CUSUM) times out of 4,096.
- **They largely missed SHIFT.** In SHIFT-4, with 156 wrong runs, the Phase II reference-based shape monitor alarmed in 1 run and the CUSUM of D in none. In SHIFT-5, with 245 wrong runs, shape alarmed in 1 and the CUSUM in 140.
- **The shape monitor alarmed more at higher LAG1 severity,** from 23 runs in LAG1-0 to 145 in LAG1-5, and alarmed in 249 runs of DUP-5.

So not every application-side signal tracks harm. Section 6.1 applies to the combined arm A, not to these monitors.

### 6.3 Bias

- **Mean CV bias in SHIFT** was negative, from −0.0033 at the lowest severity to −0.0261 at the highest.
- **In LAG1** it was positive, from 0.0070 to 0.0294.
- **In DUP** it reached 0.0565 at severity 0.5.
- **For healthy runs** it was 0.000225 and for GFSR it stayed within ±0.0008 (`report_numbers.json`).
- **The thermodynamic (TD) estimator's intervals** were wrong in all 256 runs of SHIFT-3 to -5 and DUP-4 and -5, and in at most 60 runs of any LAG1 cell (T5).

### 6.4 The pilot (descriptive only, 32 runs per severity, pilot thresholds)

The pilot covered lower severities than the campaign.
- **Where the battery began alarming in at least 31 of 32 runs:** SHIFT 0.00046, LAG1 0.029 and DUP 0.001, the lowest DUP severity tried.
- **Where the pilot wrong-rate first reached 0.5:** SHIFT 0.046, LAG1 0.5 and DUP 0.25.
- **The gap:** in all three families, the battery began alarming at a lower severity than the one where harm set in (`pilot/summary.json`).

## 7. Deviations from the plan

- **Runtime.** Generation took 110.4 minutes against a projected 83.0 (ratio 1.33, `report_numbers.json`). No result is affected.
- **Amendment 01.** It made seven analysis fixes, proposed after a code review during generation and approved by the author before any analysis. Section 5 gives its observed effect: none on any shared field.
- **DUP grid.** The frozen grid rule was applied as written, but its lower end rests on a noisy pilot count.
  - The pilot's s_lo for DUP, 0.00794, came from 4 of 32 wrong runs. The pilot's healthy runs were wrong in 9 of 199.
  - So four of the six DUP cells (DUP-0 to -3, severities up to 0.072) lie below the harm transition. The DUP family therefore has fewer cells inside its transition than intended.
- **Replay.** The first replay attempt refused to start because the single-thread BLAS settings were not set. It wrote nothing and was rerun with them. Not a change of method.
- **Scripts added after the analysis:**
  - `report_numbers.py` collects summary numbers from stored outputs only;
  - `amendment01_compare.py` was declared before the analysis.

  No post-hoc analysis was run.

## 8. What Stage C does not show

- **Scope.** One application: harmonic PIMC at one parameter point. Three synthetic word-level fault families and one real generator family. The results do not transfer to other applications or faults.
- **The battery.** Nine tests: seven from SP 800-22 and two word-level tests, on 512-sweep windows of the consumed stream. "The battery alarmed" always means this battery at a calibrated whole-run false-alarm rate of 0.05.
  - For LAG1 and DUP, two of the nine tests target the fault directly.
  - It is not the full SP 800-22 suite or TestU01.
- **Synthetic faults.** SHIFT, LAG1 and DUP say nothing about real generators.
- **GFSR.** No harm here does not mean GFSR is safe for other algorithms. The known R250 failures were with cluster algorithms ([FLW92]); this is local Metropolis PIMC. The largest 95% upper bound on a GFSR wrong-rate, 0.104, also leaves room for a small increase over the healthy 0.056.
- **Matched false alarms are calibrated marginally,** not realized exactly. V2 shows the realized rates, 0.049 for C and 0.042 for A.
- **Not H2 or H3.** No forecast or intervention was tested.

## 9. Consequences for the next stages

- **H1 as narrowed (RELATED_WORK §1) is not supported for these faults with this battery.** In every synthetic cell the online battery alarmed in every run. Among runs where both arms detected, it was earlier by a median of hundreds to thousands of sweeps. Stage C gives no evidence that reference-free application monitors add detection power over a battery that includes targeted word-level tests.
- **Where application monitoring could still matter** needs a new protocol and is an author decision:
  - faults a single-stream battery cannot see by construction, such as the channel-specific faults excluded in §9;
  - a battery without the targeted tests. Stored per-test p-values would allow a labelled post-hoc look, recalibrated on the calibration runs; a confirmatory answer needs a new protocol.
- **Stages D and E were "conditional GO, to be revisited after Stage C"** (`docs/v018/RELATED_WORK.md`). One Stage C observation bears on D (Section 6.1): the battery alarm is saturated at every severity tested, including severities below the harm class bound, while the combined application arm's alarm rate rises with severity along with the wrong-rate. Whether a graded application signal predicts remaining useful lifetime is a hypothesis for a Stage D protocol, not a Stage C result.
